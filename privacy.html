Mirage privacy policy

Mirage stores its copy of your mail on your own computer. It has no company server, no advertising, and no analytics.

Last updated: 7 November 2026

1. What Mirage reads

Mirage reads the mailboxes you connect. It uses two Google permissions:

  • gmail.modify lets it read, compose, send, label, and move mail to Trash
  • gmail.settings.basic lets it create and remove filters in Gmail when you ask

Mirage has no function that deletes mail for good. Its delete action moves mail to your provider's Trash folder. Your provider decides when to empty that folder.

2. Where your data lives

Mirage stores your mail, categories, and history in an encrypted database on your computer. Gmail, Yahoo, and iCloud sign-ins and the database key sit in Windows Credential Manager. Microsoft sign-ins sit in the encrypted database. The developer has no server and receives no copy of your data.

Mirage logs its AI, breach check, unsubscribe, webhook, and chat alert connections. The log holds counts, not email text. Encrypted backups stay on this computer, and the erase option deletes them. A retention period does not reach backups.

Attachments you save and files you export are ordinary files. They sit unencrypted in the folder you choose, and Mirage does not remove them.

Mirage keeps local mail copies until you remove them, or until they pass a retention period you set in Settings. A retention period clears those mail copies, along with their drafts and finished sends.

3. When data leaves your computer

Mirage talks to your mail provider to fetch, change, and send mail. Data reaches anyone else only in these cases, each of which you switch on or start:

  • Anthropic (AI reading). With your own API key, Mirage sends email to the Anthropic API to sort it, draft replies, summarise threads, answer your questions, and suggest categories. This includes the sender's name and address, the subject, the body, and attachment names. Proofread, Change tone, Translate, pasted notes, and your style notes also go to Anthropic. It sends a picture only when you ask it to read one. If you turn on batch reading, Anthropic keeps that data for up to 29 days. Anthropic is a United States company, so this data leaves New Zealand. Anthropic handles it under its API terms.
  • Have I Been Pwned (breach check). With your own API key, Mirage sends your connected email addresses to this service when you run a check. Breach data comes from Have I Been Pwned under a CC BY 4.0 licence.
  • Chat alerts. If you add a Slack or Teams link, Mirage sends the sender and subject of new VIP mail to it.
  • Voice input. If you turn on the microphone, your browser may send your voice to its maker to turn it into text.
  • Rules you write. Auto-send rules and your away message send email to the people you choose, or to the sender's reply address, after the wait you set.
  • Webhooks. If you add a webhook address, Mirage sends event details to that address.
  • One-click unsubscribe. When you unsubscribe, Mirage contacts the address the sender provides for that purpose.

Mirage keeps these kinds of mail away from the cloud AI: encrypted mail, mail from senders or categories you mark as local only, and mail that looks confidential. That last group covers mail marked confidential, private documents, health, legal, and financial records, identity documents, passwords, account numbers, and words you choose to protect. This check is automatic and can miss things. For local-only categories, Mirage guesses the category on your computer first, and that guess can be wrong. It judges attachments by file name only. Without an Anthropic key, a basic reader sorts your mail on your computer.

These outside services can process data outside New Zealand.

Before any other text goes to the cloud AI, Mirage tries to remove card and bank numbers, tax and health numbers, passport numbers, passwords, and keys. This is a pattern check. It can miss numbers, mainly unlabelled or overseas ones. It does not cover pictures.

The spelling and grammar check runs on your computer. The separate Proofread, Change tone, and Translate buttons use the cloud AI when you press them, if you have connected it.

The email you read and your messages can hold other people's personal information. If you use AI reading, that information goes to Anthropic with the email.

4. Google user data

Mirage's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

  • Mirage uses Gmail data only to provide the features you use
  • Mirage does not sell Gmail data
  • Mirage does not use Gmail data for advertising
  • Mirage does not use Gmail data to train general AI models
  • No person reads your Gmail data, unless you ask for help and share it yourself

5. Removing your data

  • In Mirage, open Settings and remove an account. For Gmail, this asks Google to end Mirage's access and removes that mailbox's Gmail filters. Mirage tells you if Google did not confirm. It then deletes the stored sign-in and the local mail copies. Backups keep old copies until you use the erase option. For Outlook, remove the app from your Microsoft account to end its access.
  • In Mirage, use the erase option in Settings to delete local copies of mail, drafts, history, and backups. Your sender marks stay, so a local-only mark keeps working. Saved attachments and exports stay in your folders. Connected accounts keep syncing, so recent mail returns. Remove the account first to stop this.
  • In your Google Account, open Third-party connections to remove the app's access.

7. Contact

Mirage is run by its developer, an individual in New Zealand. The developer holds none of your data. You can see, export, correct, and delete your data in the app. GitHub issues are public, so do not post email content, addresses, or account details.
